# Product Contract

## Purpose

This product is a visual daily household participation game. It is not a generic chore manager, a public social network, a financial tracker, or a system for judging family members.

Its central question is:

> What does our home need, who participated, and what did we accomplish together?

The first household has three people, including a six-year-old using a parent-managed child profile. The application must work comfortably on each person's phone and require very little daily administration.

## Core daily loop

1. A household need appears as a visual quest.
2. One or more people join it.
3. A participant marks the quest done.
4. Another household member acknowledges the completion.
5. The acknowledgement appears as gratitude, not inspection.
6. Each participant receives the full appreciation value.
7. Contribution evidence is recorded separately for household balance.
8. The quest enters the household's daily history.

Canonical quest states:

- `needed`
- `active`
- `pending_endorsement`
- `completed`
- `carried`
- `rescheduled`
- `cancelled`

## Gratitude, not surveillance

One person cannot endorse their own completion. A normal quest requires one non-participant acknowledgement. Whole-household quests can be acknowledged during the evening review.

The normal response is "I saw what you did", "Thank you", or a high five. If work needs attention, the response is "Needs a little more", with a kind explanation. There are no negative points, shame states, angry visuals, or punitive streak loss.

Corrections append history. They do not silently rewrite it.

## Cooperation

Quest types are:

- Personal
- Open
- Duo
- Family
- Care
- Surprise Help
- Big Quest
- Rescue Quest

Every participant receives the quest's full appreciation value. Contribution units are separate and divided among participants by default. This rewards cooperation without falsifying the household balance picture.

One person may record that somebody else helped. "Someone Helped" supports unscheduled kindness and care.

## Points and rewards

The underlying ledger records appreciation points. Each profile may display a different friendly name. Adults may call them Chill Points; the child may call them Watch Points.

Suitable rewards include:

- protected television time;
- ice cream, an outing, or a playdate;
- choosing dinner or the family film;
- protected alone time or hobby time;
- mutually accepted care such as a massage;
- relief from an agreed household task.

Adult intimacy is never owed, purchased, scored, or shown to a child. A future private adult connection request must require active mutual consent, allow refusal without penalty, store no explicit details, and remain outside the reward ledger.

## Living household geometry

The household is represented by a polygon:

- two members: line;
- three members: triangle;
- four members: quadrilateral;
- five members: pentagon;
- larger households: adaptive polygon or constellation.

Each vertex represents a member. Edges hold shared work between people. The centre holds whole-household quests. High fives can travel along the edges.

The shape responds to verified contribution units compared with agreed contribution targets over a rolling seven-day window. Targets account for age, ability, health, school, work, time, and custody. Adults may inspect detailed ratios. Children see only supportive household language and are never identified as the cause of imbalance.

## Home Dinosaur and Home Energy

An original young triceratops is the household companion. It lives with the family shape and helps a child understand the game through expression, posture, movement, and sound.

The dinosaur responds to household states: resting, noticing a need, welcoming a participant, encouraging cooperation, holding completed energy while work waits for thanks, celebrating appreciation, and sleeping during quiet hours.

The dinosaur represents the household as a whole. It may never become hungry, sick, frightened, neglected, disappointed, or distressed because work remains unfinished. It does not compare people or communicate blame.

Verified completion creates non-spendable Home Energy. Home Energy belongs to the household, visually strengthens the polygon and companion habitat, and remains separate from personal appreciation points. It never decreases as punishment.

## Child experience

The child profile is parent-managed and does not require an email address. The child must be able to:

- recognise their avatar;
- understand quests through icons and optional spoken instructions;
- join a quest;
- mark it done;
- receive thanks and high fives;
- understand available child-safe rewards.

The child view must avoid adult financial, relationship, private-note, and detailed household-balance information.

## Gentle urgency

Unfinished quests become more noticeable, like something patiently asking for care. They may move closer to the centre, softly wiggle, or use a gentle sound. They may not shame a person, deduct points, interrupt quiet hours, or become frightening.

An evening review gathers thanks, unfinished work, carry-over decisions, and tomorrow's preparation.

## Low-administration rules

The system supports daily, weekly, selected-day, every-N-day, monthly, and when-needed recurrence. It automatically generates the day's quests, rotates suitable work, archives history, and offers quick add, repeat yesterday, and reusable household templates.

The normal home screen is Today. The full task library belongs in adult management and must not dominate daily use.

## Multiple households

Every private record belongs to a `household_id`. Friends create independent households with their own people, language, quests, schedules, rewards, targets, quiet hours, and acknowledgement rules.

Shareable template packs may contain generic quest ideas, icons, defaults, and reward ideas. They never contain names, history, balances, completions, private notes, or personal data.

The first release serves one household. A trusted friend household follows only after the first household has used the system successfully.

## Explicit exclusions

The first release does not include:

- finances, expenses, maintenance, or cost splitting;
- public profiles, public leaderboards, or public social interaction;
- advertisements;
- subscriptions or a marketplace;
- native iOS or Android applications;
- AI-generated household decisions;
- sexual rewards;
- negative scores or punishment mechanics.
